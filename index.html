Mirante Residences: should we buy the land?

A developer has an option on a site zoned for a 72-unit mid-rise with 5,400 m² of sellable area (about 58,100 sq ft). Before paying the deposit, the partners need to know the return on their equity, how much cash they must put in and when they get it back.

What the model answers

  • Profit, margin on GDV and profit on cost after financing
  • Peak equity the partners must fund, and in which month
  • Equity IRR against an 18% hurdle, with and without debt
  • How far sale price and construction cost can move before the deal stops working

Development budget

Monthly, not annual

Land, soft costs, a sine-shaped construction S-curve and buyer payments all land in the month they happen. Annual models hide the peak equity, and the peak is what the partners are actually signing up for.

Payment plans drive cash

Buyers pay a deposit at signing, monthly installments until handover and the balance at completion. Units sold after completion pay in full on sale. This is how off-plan sales work in Brazil and most of Latin America.

Debt with real mechanics

The construction loan funds a share of each hard-cost draw, interest is capitalized monthly and every dollar received after completion sweeps to repay it before equity sees a distribution.

Linden Court: what is the most we should pay?

A 24-unit apartment building is listed at US$4.0M. The investor will finance it with a bank loan, hold for five years and sell. The question is the bid, not just the IRR at the asking price.

What the model answers

  • Loan amount sized by whichever binds first: loan-to-value or the lender's minimum DSCR
  • Levered and unlevered IRR, equity multiple and year-1 cash-on-cash
  • The maximum purchase price that still clears the investor's required return
  • Exit value on forward NOI, for any hold period from 3 to 10 years

Debt sized like a lender would

The loan is the lower of max LTV on the purchase price and the amount year-1 NOI can carry at the minimum DSCR. The model tells you which test binds.

Exit on forward NOI

The sale price capitalizes the NOI of the year after the sale, net of selling costs and the remaining loan balance from a monthly amortization schedule.

Hurdle you can trace

The required return is built from a risk-free rate, beta and equity risk premium plus a property-specific premium, and the model solves for the price where levered IRR equals it.

Northfield Packaging: what is the business worth?

The owners of a US$42M-revenue packaging manufacturer are talking to a buyer. They need a five-year forecast the buyer's analysts can audit and a valuation range they can defend.

What the model answers

  • Income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement, linked so the balance sheet balances every year
  • Unlevered free cash flow, enterprise value and equity value
  • Implied EV/EBITDA, as a sanity check against market multiples
  • How much of the value sits in the terminal value

Three statements, one engine

Working capital runs on days (DSO, DIO, DPO), PP&E rolls forward with capex and depreciation, and cash is the plug from the cash flow statement. The balance check row has to read zero, and it does.

Valuation from the same numbers

Free cash flow comes straight from the projected statements: EBIT after tax, plus D&A, minus capex and the change in working capital. Nothing is typed in twice.

Built to be challenged

The implied multiple and the terminal value share sit next to the answer, because those are the first two things a buyer's analyst will question.

Harbor & Pine Supply: a 13-week cash flow that builds itself

A building-materials distributor rebuilt its cash forecast by hand every Monday from five ERP exports. This Python script now does it in seconds, using each customer's actual payment history instead of the due dates on the invoices.

What the script delivers

  • Weekly receipts and disbursements for 13 weeks, by category
  • Revolver draws and repayments that hold a minimum cash balance
  • Automatic flags: cash shortfalls, stale receivables, slow payers, concentration
